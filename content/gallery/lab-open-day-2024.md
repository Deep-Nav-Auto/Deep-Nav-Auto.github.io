Once a year we open the lab doors, wipe the whiteboards, and try to explain what we do without assuming everyone has taken a graduate course in estimation theory. Fall 2024 was our largest turnout yet: prospective MSc and PhD students, a few high-school visitors, and colleagues from geomatics and electrical engineering who wanted to see the hardware in person.

We set up stations—GNSS replay on a laptop, a spinning LiDAR point cloud on the projector, a poster on radar–vision fusion, and the roof-rack rig under good lighting so people could ask “how much does this cost?” without whispering. The honest answer is “less than you fear, more than you budget,” and that usually starts a useful conversation about scope.

---

The best questions were not technical. They were about how we choose projects, how often we go to the field, and whether students get to drive the van (yes, after training). By the end we had a stack of contact cards and a group photo that will inevitably appear on this gallery page.

If you missed it, reach out through the contact page—we would rather show you the lab on a quiet Tuesday than have you guess from the website alone.
