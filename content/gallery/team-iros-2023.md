Detroit in early October means packed sessions, long walks between halls, and the particular energy of a robotics community that has not fully reunited since the pandemic years. Several of us made the trip to present work on sensor fusion and to scout ideas at the boundary of navigation, perception, and learning.

The week was dense. We caught keynotes on trustworthy autonomy, argued about factor-graph formulations over coffee, and spent an evening at a restaurant near the river debriefing which demos were reproducible and which were theatre. Students who had only seen conference names on paper met collaborators they will email for years.

---

For the lab, IROS is as much about calibration as publication. We returned with a list of benchmarks to try, a handful of new contacts for joint field tests, and the usual collection of badge lanyards that end up on the lab whiteboard until someone finally throws them out.

If you are planning your first major conference, our advice is simple: schedule one open afternoon. The hallway conversations you think you will skip are often the ones that change your project.
